Project Abstract

Early detection of malignant cutaneous lesions significantly improves patient prognostic outcomes. Conventional computer-assisted diagnosis relies primarily on single-frame spatial analysis, often ignoring critical temporal dynamics. This research proposes a multi-stage decision-support framework integrating deep local feature extraction via Convolutional Neural Networks (EfficientNet-B4) and long-range contextual spatial attention via Vision Transformers (ViT-Base).

Furthermore, the system incorporates TEM-Seg (Task-Guided Efficient Medical Segmentation) to delineate precise lesion perimeters and utilizes a Siamese spatial registration network for longitudinal comparison. By comparing sequential skin images, the system calculates differential indicators across asymmetric axis shifts, border spiculation, and surface area delta, presenting clinicians with intuitive Grad-CAM heatmaps and ViT self-attention grids.
